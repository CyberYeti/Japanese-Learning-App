# How Fluyo's beginner lessons are structured

Research for [#15](https://github.com/CyberYeti/Japanese-Learning-App/issues/15), part of the Beginner Track map [#14](https://github.com/CyberYeti/Japanese-Learning-App/issues/14). Researched 2026-10-27.

The question was what Fluyo's Japanese beginner curriculum looks like: lesson sequence, how much one lesson introduces, how verbs, conjugations and particles are ordered, how vocabulary is introduced, and how examples stay within known words and kanji. The aim is a reference for ordering and step size, not content to copy.

## Short answer

Fluyo does not publish a curriculum, syllabus, or lesson list for Japanese (or any language). Its website, App Store and Play Store listings describe features, not a sequence. Everything below about the actual order comes from screen recordings of the app, the founder's own statements, and user reviews. Only the first few Japanese beginner lessons are visible in those sources. **A full lesson-by-lesson outline could not be verified**, and getting one would mean playing through the app and writing it down by hand.

What can be established:

1. **Lessons are small and tied to a short word set.** Each step of the Journey path either teaches a handful of words ("learn words") or teaches one grammar point in a short narrated audio lesson (a "wayfinder lesson"). Reviewers describe "a handful of words and sentences per level" and about four new words per battle segment.
2. **Japanese starts with kana, then verbs.** The course opens with an overview of the three writing systems, then hiragana and katakana taught with mnemonics, each new kana immediately used in a few words. The first grammar lesson is about verbs: a bare dictionary-form verb is already a full sentence ("I eat"), the subject can be left out, and the same form covers present and future. The first word set after that is common verbs (to come, to sleep, to eat, to go home, to go).
3. **Design intent is "grammar using words you already know".** The founder describes the goal as learning the most common ~3,000 words and "master the key grammar points using those same exact words". That is the same known-words rule the Beginner Track wants.
4. **In practice the rule leaks.** Several reviews say quizzes and lessons use words or grammar that haven't been taught yet. Fluyo is a reference for the intended design, not proof that the design works.
5. **Kanji are handled inconsistently.** In the Journey lessons, words and example sentences show kanji. The built-in flashcard decks show the target word in kana only, even for words almost always written in kanji (私, 猫, 傘). No source describes a kanji introduction order or a furigana policy.
6. **Only the beginner level exists for Japanese.** Higher difficulty levels say "new content soon" and Journey is locked there. Development has been paused since 2025, with a small contract team on bug fixes, so the beginner course is unlikely to grow.

## Course shape

**Journey is the course.** The Journey path is a Duolingo-style map of steps grouped into areas (the first Japanese area after kana is "Sandy Beach"). A step is one of:

- **Wayfinder lesson.** A short narrated grammar lesson. A character explains one point in English, with an on-screen transcript and play/pause. The learner answers comprehension questions by swiping a choice, and wrong choices come with an explanation. The founder calls these "grammar audio lessons" and "literally the meat of our courses" ([Evildea 2025, ~861–866s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g3LSYenLFiU)). Reviewers single these out as the best part: "the wayfinder lessons have explained concepts that I've struggled with" (Play Store review); one explained "a Korean simple grammar rule about conjugation" and "reinforced grammar rules that I've already learned" ([Diana Lingual, ~222–286s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RG5wez5aDXc)).
- **Learn words.** A small set of new words. Each word is shown with its meaning and several example sentences, "from easy to more complex" ([founder walkthrough, ~158–171s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pD3RZZkDho4)), followed by matching and fill-in exercises.
- **Battle.** A creature fight where each move is a quiz on the words just learned. Reviewers agree it is a review drill: "all this really is is just a way for you to review a vocab" ([Mike 2025b, ~514–518s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dGqd1e_dErk)).

Every word learned in Journey goes into an automatic "journey deck" of spaced-repetition flashcards, and the mini-games draw from those decks ([founder walkthrough, ~360–418s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pD3RZZkDho4)). Separate themed decks (basic words, media, emotions, and so on) are not linked to the lesson order.

**Step size.** No source gives a fixed count. The observable sizes:

- One grammar point per wayfinder lesson. Every recording shows one topic per lesson.
- About four new words per battle segment ([Days and Words, ~647–657s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GaXAzL3HGl4)). Five verbs in the first Japanese word set ([Mike 2025b, ~359–406s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dGqd1e_dErk)). "A handful of words and sentences per level" ([Kevin Abroad 2026, ~1205–1215s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4zCqCOes0C8)).
- The daily quest is "learn 10 new words" ([Mike 2025b, ~632s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dGqd1e_dErk)). Free users get two lessons a day ([Days and Words, ~101s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GaXAzL3HGl4)).
- An App Store release note (v1.3.0) mentions cutting some per-lesson count from 150 to 50. The note is garbled, so what was being counted could not be confirmed.

## The Japanese beginner sequence, as far as it can be seen

From the two Mike Learns Japanese recordings (Japanese, beginner level):

1. **Writing-system overview.** Japanese uses three scripts, with a short intro to what hiragana and katakana are for ([Mike 2025a, ~351–627s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UjvBJSGo-PM)). The release notes confirm that wayfinder lessons include hiragana and katakana for Japanese (v1.2.3).
2. **Kana, one sound at a time, with mnemonics and immediate words.** The recording shows a mnemonic for one kana, then a question asking which word contains it, then a word built only from kana already taught ([Mike 2025a, ~731–865s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UjvBJSGo-PM)). The order does not follow the あいうえお table: the first kana shown was し. The reviewer found that odd.
3. **First grammar lesson: verbs on their own.** An "explanation of verbs" lesson ([Mike 2025b, ~202–298s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dGqd1e_dErk)) makes three points:
   - The dictionary form of a verb such as 食べる can stand alone as a sentence meaning "I eat". No subject is needed and the word doesn't change.
   - The same unchanged form covers present and future (non-past).
   - Who is doing it comes from context. A quiz shows one bare verb and asks which reading is right; the answer is that all of them are possible depending on context.
4. **First word set: common verbs.** The next step ("Sandy Beach – learn words") teaches 来る, 寝る, 食べる, 帰る and 行く, shown with kanji ([Mike 2025b, ~359–406s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dGqd1e_dErk)).
5. **Battle reviewing those verbs** ([Mike 2025b, ~487–601s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dGqd1e_dErk)).

Neither recording goes further. **Not verified:** when particles (は, を, に, etc.) come in, the order of conjugations (polite ます forms, negatives, past, て-form), when adjectives and です come in, and how long the beginner course is. The founder says Japanese, Korean, German, Spanish and French have "robust content for basically beginner" ([Evildea 2025, ~883–887s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g3LSYenLFiU)), but the size is not published.

The one structural point worth borrowing: **Fluyo teaches verbs before particles and before conjugation.** The learner first meets the bare verb as a whole sentence, then gets a set of high-frequency verbs to use with it. Later grammar can then be layered onto words the learner already has. That is the opposite of Tae Kim's Basic Grammar, which opens with the state-of-being (だ/です), then particles and adjectives, and only then reaches verbs, without ever teaching a set of core verbs as vocabulary.

## Vocabulary introduction

- New words are introduced in Journey "learn words" steps, a few at a time. Each comes with meaning, audio, and several example sentences graded from simple to more complex ([founder walkthrough](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pD3RZZkDho4)).
- Some words have attached "remenies", short funny voice clips meant as memory aids ([founder walkthrough, ~173–180s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pD3RZZkDho4)).
- Grammar lessons reuse words from earlier word sets: the verb lesson uses 食べる, and the next word set then teaches it formally.
- Words move into the learner's spaced-repetition deck as they are learned. This matches this app's Mark complete step, which turns lesson words into items.
- Reviewers criticised the word choice at higher levels (overly formal expressions, cognate-heavy lessons, single-word translations that don't fit the example sentence) ([Kevin Abroad 2026, ~1259–1278s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4zCqCOes0C8); [Days and Words, ~330–426s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GaXAzL3HGl4)). One reviewer guessed the courses are levelled the same way across languages ([Days and Words, ~466–509s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GaXAzL3HGl4)). That is speculation, not confirmed.

## Keeping examples within known words and kanji

**Intent.** The founder's stated design is to learn the most common ~3,000 words "quickly" and "master the key grammar points using those same exact words" ([Evildea 2025, ~211–217s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g3LSYenLFiU)), and the 2023 pitch promised "the most common one thousand to two thousand words" within months ([founder, part 3, ~42–46s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fz8OWuQFqwk)). Grammar is meant to be taught and practised on the learner's own word set.

**Practice.** The rule is not reliably enforced. Several Play Store reviews independently report:

- "you get quizzed on words the app hasn't even taught you yet"
- "in a few lessons and exercises grammar that has not been taught is introduced"
- "the app asks for things that it hasn't taught yet (like word genders)"
- "lessons skipping grammar … had to guess because I didn't know verb conjugations" (French)

So the known-words rule looks like an authoring guideline rather than something the app checks. The Beginner Track should not copy that gap: this app already has tokenizer-based checks (Coverage, Matchers) that could test lesson examples against the known-word list.

**Kanji.** There is no published kanji policy.

- Journey words and example sentences show kanji (来る, 食べる, etc.) ([Mike 2025b, ~212–223s, ~363s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dGqd1e_dErk)).
- The themed flashcard decks show the headword in kana only, while the example sentence on the same card may contain kanji. This was criticised in both Mike recordings and by Kevin Abroad ([Mike 2025a, ~1164–1224s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UjvBJSGo-PM); [Mike 2025b, ~738–875s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dGqd1e_dErk); [Kevin Abroad 2025, ~352–466s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rBsC97RQUYc)).
- Kevin Abroad reported example sentences with kanji he couldn't read and no reading shown ([~352–364s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rBsC97RQUYc)). That suggests no consistent furigana, at least in the decks.
- **Not verified:** whether kanji in Journey are limited to ones already taught, or whether furigana is shown there.

Fluyo is no help on the map's open question about how kanji enter the track. It shows the failure to avoid: kanji in example sentences that the learner was never taught to read.

## Status of the product

- The App Store and Play Store listings still say Japanese has "full support". In the app, only the beginner level has Journey content; Intermediate, Upper Intermediate and Advanced show "new content soon" with Journey locked ([Mike 2025b, ~1039–1079s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dGqd1e_dErk); [Mike 2025a, ~228–247s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UjvBJSGo-PM)).
- By late 2025 the founder's update said new development was paused and only "a very small team" on contract would keep the app online and fix bugs ([Evildea, Nov 2025, ~451–468s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JFYarMVmScQ)). The October 2026 retrospective describes the app as stable but "bare-bones" ([Kevin Abroad 2026, ~1187–1222s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4zCqCOes0C8)).

## What this means for the Beginner Track

These are notes for [#17](https://github.com/CyberYeti/Japanese-Learning-App/issues/17) ("Import, author, or blend"), not decisions:

- **Nothing to import.** Fluyo's content is proprietary, unpublished, and frozen. It can only shape the structure.
- **Structural pattern worth matching:** one grammar point per lesson, paired with a small word set (about 4–10 words), and words learned in a lesson feed straight into spaced repetition. This matches the map's "lessons teach, passages practice" note and Mark complete creating items.
- **Ordering signal:** kana → bare verbs as whole sentences (subject dropped, non-past) → a batch of core verbs → grammar layered on those verbs. Only the first three steps are verified. The rest of the order (particles, ます, negatives, past, て-form) needs another source, such as an openly licensed beginner syllabus or JLPT N5 grammar lists.
- **Known-words rule:** Fluyo intends it but doesn't enforce it, and users notice when it breaks. Enforcing it with the existing tokenizer and coverage tools would be an improvement on Fluyo, not a copy.
- **Kanji:** Fluyo offers no usable model. The track will need its own policy.

## Gaps

- No lesson list, unit count, or total word count for the Japanese beginner course was found from any source.
- The order of particles, conjugations, adjectives and copula in Fluyo's Japanese course could not be verified past the first verb lesson.
- No source says whether Journey example sentences are checked against taught words or kanji.
- The fluyoapp.com domain had an invalid certificate and the Kickstarter page returned 403, so neither could be checked. Reddit (r/Fluyo) blocked automated access.
- Closing these gaps would mean playing through the beginner Journey and logging each step's grammar point and word list by hand.
